      How the numbers are calculated

      TTM (last 12 months) adds up the last four reported quarters. Latest quarter ends —; latest fiscal year ends —.
      P/E = market cap ÷ TTM net profit. Market cap = latest close × shares outstanding on the latest balance sheet.
      ROE = TTM net profit ÷ average shareholders’ equity (now and a year ago), the standard way to match a year of profit with the equity that earned it.
      ROCE = TTM EBIT ÷ average capital employed, where capital employed = total assets − current liabilities.
      Debt (borrowings) leaves out lease obligations, as US accounting rules class operating leases as operating items. Rating agencies often count leases as debt, so debt with leases is shown too.
      Free cash flow = cash from operations − capital spending. Growth is compounded per year (CAGR) over fiscal years. EV = market cap + borrowings − cash.
      Industry comes from the company’s FMP profile, matched to the closest benchmark industry by keyword. Industry figures: P/E is total market cap ÷ total profit of the industry’s profitable firms; margins and ROE are industry aggregates (Damodaran, January 2026).
      Colors are rules of thumb, not advice: compare within an industry, and treat red as “look closer,” not “sell.”
      Data check: — gap between the latest fiscal year’s sales and the sum of its four quarters.

      Live bot rules (one page)

      The only bot trading real money. Robinhood agentic (MCP) account, about $100. v3.2 live since Nov 25 2026 (v3.0 Nov 23–24, v1 before). Research tool, not financial advice.

      Live nowv3.2

      Before thatv3.0 (Nov 23–24)

      1. When it runs

      • Every trading day at 3:47 pm ET (backup 3:52; 12:47 on 1 pm half days), from an Oracle Cloud VM.
      • v3.2 checks every 15 minutes from 10:00 am ET to 20 minutes before the close (real orders; alerts only when it trades).
      • Health check 8:45 am (alerts only on problems); fill check 4:35 pm.
      • One run at a time (a lock stops overlaps).

      2. What it buys

      • Stocks: S&P 500, above its 200-day average, fell 10% or more worse than SPY in 5 days.
      • Skipped if the 5-day volume is more than 2× normal (usually real bad news).
      • ETFs (new in v3.0): 29 index / sector / country ETFs down 5%+ in 5 days and above their 200-day, only in slots the stocks leave free. Never today's parking ETF.
      • Biggest dips first, up to 10 positions, 10% each.
      • v3.2: the same buys can happen during the day instead of waiting for 3:47, paid from cash, then by selling some of the parked ETF.

      3. When it sells

      • The first day it closes above its 5-day average, or
      • after 5 trading days (ETFs 10) even at a loss; the backtest counts every such loss.
      • v3.2: also sells as soon as it is +5% over the buy price during the day, only if held at least 1 day (never a same-day round trip).
      • No stop-loss (tests showed stops hurt).

      4. Idle money

      • SPY above its 100-day average: the Clear Leader ETF.
      • SPY below it: IEF bonds if IEF is above its own 100-day average (new in v3.0), otherwise cash. This is the crash defense.

      5. Safety rules

      • Sells first, then buys sized to real buying power. Never margin.
      • Dip orders max 20% of the account; parking orders max the account value; strategy symbols only; never sells more than it holds.
      • Never buys or sells the same symbol twice in a day; a retried order can't double (same order key).
      • Stops itself on unknown orders, failed safety checks, an expired login, or a 40% drop from the peak (MAX_DRAWDOWN=0.40 in the VM config).

      6. Alerts and your job

      • Phone alerts (ntfy): trades and fills normal; failures high; login expired / halted / unknown orders urgent. Errors alert once a day, not every 15 minutes.
      • On the VM: dip status (check), dip preview / preview-intraday (today's plan, places nothing), dip halt / dip resume (pause), dip login (when it says the login expired).
